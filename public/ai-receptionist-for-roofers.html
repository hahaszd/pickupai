It asks what a roofer would ask

Most answering services take a name and a number. That leaves you ringing back to find out whether it's a tarp job tonight or a re-roof next month. PickupAI asks the roofing questions on the call:

  • Is there active water coming in right now? The difference between tonight and next week.
  • Was this triggered by a recent storm, or has it been happening for a while? Tells you whether it's a genuine storm callout or a long-running leak.
  • What type of roof — tiles, Colorbond/metal, or other? So you know what you're dealing with before you load the van.
  • Roughly how old is the roof? An old roof and a five-year-old roof are different conversations.

It also gives one practical safety tip. On an active leak it tells the caller to avoid the rooms directly under it until the roof's inspected — ceilings can become waterlogged and heavy, and that's the one thing worth saying before you get there.

It still takes details when the caller's outside your usual area, rather than turning them away or guessing your radius. After a localised hail event, calls come from further afield than normal — the AI captures them and flags the address as out of area for you to decide.

Emergencies get flagged, and the rest don't

A receptionist that marks everything urgent is one you stop reading. PickupAI flags a lead as an emergency when the caller describes a roof collapse, active flooding through the ceiling, structural damage, or storm damage — and sends a second text a couple of minutes later if you haven't rung back yet.

Everything else — a quote request, a maintenance check — comes through as a normal job, so the word EMERGENCY still means something after a big blow-through.

What it won't do

It won't quote. Ever. A roofing price depends on what's actually wrong up there, and a robot's guess becomes a number you're held to at the door. It gets the job details and leaves the pricing to you.

It won't promise an arrival time, especially in a storm. A roof can't be safely worked on in live rain, so promising a time you can't keep just moves the argument from the phone to your driveway. It takes the details and leaves timing to you.

It won't pretend to be human. If a caller asks, it says it's an AI assistant, and it discloses it at the end of every call regardless. Callers who find out later feel tricked; callers who are told plainly mostly don't care, because someone finally answered.
