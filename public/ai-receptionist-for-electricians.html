It asks what an electrician would ask

Most answering services take a name and a number. That leaves you ringing back to find out whether it's a two-minute reset or a genuine hazard. PickupAI asks the electrical questions on the call:

  • Is it a complete power outage or only part of the house? Tells you the size of the job before you load the van.
  • Has the circuit breaker tripped? Have they tried resetting it? Rules out the simplest fix over the phone.
  • Any burning smell, sparks, or visible damage? The difference between a callback tomorrow and get-away-and-call-000 tonight.
  • Is it safe to be in the affected area right now? So you know whether the caller needs to move before anything else.

It matches the safety advice to what's actually happening. A plain outage with no smell, smoke or damage gets different guidance from one appliance being the obvious culprit, which gets different guidance again from smoke or sparking at the switchboard. See what it won't do below for exactly where the line is drawn.

Emergencies get flagged, and the rest don't

A receptionist that treats every enquiry as urgent is one you stop reading. PickupAI flags a lead as an emergency when the caller describes sparks, a burning smell, an electrical fire, a power outage, a shock, or a live wire — and sends a second text a couple of minutes later if you haven't rung back yet.

Everything else — a power point that's stopped working, a light fitting that needs replacing — comes through as a normal job, so the word EMERGENCY still means something at 2am.

What it won't do

It won't quote. Ever. An electrical price depends on what's actually wrong, and a robot's guess becomes a number you're held to at the door. It gets the job details and leaves the pricing to you.

It won't tell a caller to open or operate a switchboard. If someone describes smoke, sparking, buzzing or heat at the switchboard, meter box or a power point, it tells them to get away from it now and call 000 — not to go near it, let alone touch anything.

It won't send someone who's just taken a shock back to the switchboard. It tells them to leave the appliance or switch alone, to see a doctor that day even if they feel fine, and to call 000 straight away if they're feeling faint, short of breath or their heart's racing.

It won't book you a callout for a fault that isn't yours. If the caller's neighbours have lost power too, it tells them the outage is the electricity network's problem, not something you attend — so you're not driving out to a job you can't bill for.

It won't pretend to be human. If a caller asks, it says it's an AI assistant, and it discloses it at the end of every call regardless. Callers who find out later feel tricked; callers who are told plainly mostly don't care, because someone finally answered.

Common questions from electricians

Can it tell a dangerous fault from a simple power outage?

Yes. It listens for sparks, a burning smell, an electrical fire, a power outage, a shock or a live wire and flags the lead as an emergency so it stands out in your texts. A power point that's just stopped working with no smell, smoke or damage comes through as a normal job.

What does it do if someone's had a shock?

It tells them not to touch the appliance or switch again, that anyone who's taken a shock from mains power should see a doctor that day even if they feel fine, and to hang up and call 000 if they feel faint, short of breath, or their heart is racing. It never tells someone who's just been shocked to go back to the switchboard.

What does it ask an electrical caller?

Whether it's a complete power outage or only part of the house, whether the circuit breaker has tripped and whether they've tried resetting it, whether there's any burning smell, sparks or visible damage, and whether it's safe to be in the affected area right now.

What if I answer the phone myself?

The AI only picks up when you don't. Call forwarding kicks in after about 20 seconds of ringing, so if you get to it first the AI never activates.
