It asks what a plumber would ask

Most answering services take a name and a number. That leaves you ringing back to find out whether you need to drop everything. PickupAI asks the plumbing questions on the call:

  • Is water actively leaking right now? The difference between tonight and Thursday.
  • Hot or cold water? Tells you whether it's a hot water unit before you load the van.
  • Can they see where it's coming from, or is it hidden? A hidden leak is a different job.
  • One fixture or several areas? Separates a single blockage from something in the main line.
  • Owner or tenant — does the landlord need contacting? So you know who authorises the work before you turn up.

It also gives one practical safety tip. On a burst pipe it tells the caller to turn the water off at the mains near the meter — which stops damage while they wait, and means the job you arrive to is smaller than the one they rang about.

Emergencies get flagged, and the rest don't

A receptionist that marks everything urgent is one you stop reading. PickupAI flags a lead as an emergency when the caller describes a burst pipe, flooding, sewage overflow, or a drain backing up — and sends a second text a couple of minutes later if you haven't rung back yet.

Everything else comes through as a normal job, so the word EMERGENCY still means something at 2am.

What it won't do

It won't quote. Ever. A plumbing price depends on what's behind the wall, and a robot's guess becomes a number you're held to at the door. It gets the job details and leaves the pricing to you.

It won't promise you'll attend a gas job. Gas fitting is separately licensed. If a caller reports a gas smell, the AI tells them to leave the building and ring 000 before anything else — then still takes their details so you can ring back and decide.

It won't pretend to be human. If a caller asks, it says it's an AI assistant, and it discloses it at the end of every call regardless. Callers who find out later feel tricked; callers who are told plainly mostly don't care, because someone finally answered.

Common questions from plumbers

Can it tell a burst pipe from a dripping tap?

Yes. It listens for the things that make a plumbing job urgent — burst pipe, flooding, sewage overflow, a blocked drain backing up — and flags the lead as an emergency so it stands out in your texts. A dripping tap comes through as a normal job.

Does it do gas work enquiries?

It takes the enquiry, but it will never tell a caller you'll attend a gas job. If someone reports a gas smell, it tells them to leave the building and ring 000 first, then still captures their details so you can ring back. Whether you take gas work is your call, not the AI's.

What does it ask a plumbing caller?

Whether water is actively leaking right now, whether it is a hot or cold water issue, whether they can see where it is coming from, whether it affects one fixture or several, and whether they own the place or are renting and the landlord needs contacting.

What if I answer the phone myself?

The AI only picks up when you don't. Call forwarding kicks in after about 20 seconds of ringing, so if you get to it first the AI never activates.
