It asks what a plumber would ask

Most answering services take a name and a number. That leaves you ringing back to find out whether you need to drop everything. PickupAI asks the plumbing questions on the call:

  • Is water actively leaking right now? The difference between tonight and Thursday.
  • Hot or cold water? Tells you whether it's a hot water unit before you load the van.
  • Can they see where it's coming from, or is it hidden? A hidden leak is a different job.
  • One fixture or several areas? Separates a single blockage from something in the main line.
  • Owner or tenant — does the landlord need contacting? So you know who authorises the work before you turn up.

It does not give advice, and it asks nothing of the caller. Someone standing in a flooding kitchen does not need a machine that cannot see the room to start issuing instructions. It takes the details and gets off the line, because the fastest useful thing it can do is put the call in front of you.

It writes it down. You decide.

PickupAI does not sort your jobs for you. It has never seen your run, your suburb or your Tuesday — so it writes down what the caller actually said, word for word, and puts that in your hand seconds after they hang up.

Reading it takes you two seconds, and nobody had to guess on your behalf.

What it won't do

It won't quote. Ever. A plumbing price depends on what's behind the wall, and a robot's guess becomes a number you're held to at the door. It gets the job details and leaves the pricing to you.

It won't promise you'll attend a gas job. Gas fitting is separately licensed. A caller who reports a gas smell hears one line — that this sounds like one for triple zero — and is asked for their number in the same breath. It does not tell them to leave the building or open a window; it takes the details so you can ring back, and whether you take gas work is your call.

It won't pretend to be human. If a caller asks, it says it's an AI assistant, and it discloses it at the end of every call regardless. Callers who find out later feel tricked; callers who are told plainly mostly don't care, because someone finally answered.
